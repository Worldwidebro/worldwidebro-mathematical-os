### Post 2: Thought Leadership
**Title:** "Cap Rate vs. Cash Flow: Why Most Investors Are Looking at the Wrong Number"

Real estate investors often fixate on cap rate. It's the wrong metric.

**Cap rate = (Annual NOI) / (Purchase price)**

For a $120K house with $1,200/month cash flow = 12% cap rate. Looks great.

**But cash flow is what matters.** Why?

- Cap rate assumes immediate sale (you're not selling)
- Cap rate ignores financing (you're using a mortgage, not cash)
- Cap rate is a snapshot (doesn't account for appreciation + principal paydown)
- **Cash flow is real money in your pocket every month**

**The mental shift:** Stop asking "what's the cap rate?" Start asking "what's the monthly cash flow after all expenses?"

For a $120K purchase, 12% cap rate = $1,200/month cash flow = $14,400/year in actual money. That's retirement income.

### Post 3: Problem/Solution
**Title:** "Why Finding Off-Market Deals Actually Saves You Money"

Myth: Off-market deals are a luxury for experienced investors.

Reality: Off-market deals are the only sustainable path to real cash flow.

**Why?**
- Market-listed properties are bid up 15-25% above true value
- You're competing with 50 other buyers (price goes up)
- Cap rates on MLS properties = 3-5% (low yield)
- Off-market properties = owner-distressed, seller motivated
- Off-market properties = 8-12% cap rate potential

**The math:** Buying on market = overpay + low yield. Buying off-market = fair price + high yield. Same property, wildly different outcomes.

### Post 5: Tip/Insight
**Title:** "The 3 Numbers Every Real Estate Investor Should Know (And Calculate First)"

Don't fall for shiny marketing about properties. Know these 3 numbers first:

1. **Acquisition cost (true cost, not MLS list)**
   - MLS price often 15-25% above fair value
   - Off-market deals closer to fair value

2. **Monthly cash flow after all expenses**
   - Mortgage + insurance + taxes + maintenance reserve + vacancy reserve
   - This is the money you actually keep monthly

3. **Capitalization rate (for comparison only)**
   - 8%+ cap rate = good deal
   - 3-5% cap rate = over-priced (MLS syndrome)

**If you don't know these 3 numbers before making an offer, you'll overpay.**

### Post 6: Problem/Solution
**Title:** "The $15K Mistake: Property Management Incompetence"

Investor buys property for cash flow. Then hires a property manager who:
- Doesn't screen tenants properly (evictions, bad tenants)
- Underprices rent (leaves money on table)
- Lets properties deteriorate (deferred maintenance)
- Doesn't handle leases professionally (tenant disputes)

**Cost:** 1-2 bad tenants = $10K in eviction costs + lost rent + repairs. Incompetent management kills cash flow.

**Solution:** Professional property management (8-10% of rent) >> self-management (zero cost + hidden loss of $5K-$10K/year in bad tenants + deferred maintenance).
