### Post 2: Thought Leadership (Week 1, Thu)
**Title:** "Why Licensed Construction Matters (It's Not Just a Badge)"

Licensing exists for one reason: compliance. But compliance isn't a checkbox—it's a guarantee.

When a contractor is bonded and licensed, you're not paying extra for paperwork. You're paying for:
- Insurance covering defects and delays (your protection)
- Third-party verification of qualifications (not self-reported)
- Legal recourse if something goes wrong (bonded performance guarantee)
- Weekly compliance audits during execution (not post-mortem inspections)

Every $1 you "save" with an unlicensed contractor typically costs $3 in rework, code violations, or delayed closeout.

**The ask:** When hiring next time, ask three things: (1) License verified? (2) Insurance current? (3) Who inspects the work?

### Post 3: Problem/Solution (Week 2, Tue)
**Title:** "Subcontractor No-Shows Are Killing Your Profit Margin"

General contractors: how much margin did you lose last quarter to subcontractor delays?

Typical answer: 15-25% of project profit. Why?
- Subs schedule themselves for 3 jobs simultaneously
- One job runs over → all downstream jobs slip
- Your margin window closes → you're now liable for late delivery
- Customer costs spike → customer satisfaction tanks
- You can't recover the margin from the customer because it was a fixed-price quote

**The real cost:** 1 delayed sub = margin loss on entire project chain.

**The solution:** Pre-vetted subcontractor network. No juggling. No surprises. Same crew every time = predictable timelines = protected margins.

### Post 5: Tip/Insight (Week 3, Tue)
**Title:** "How to Verify a Contractor's License in NC (30 seconds)"

NC General Contractor License verification:
1. Go to ncsu.edu/licensing
2. Search contractor name
3. Check: License status (Active/Inactive), Expiration date, Complaint history
4. Call 3 references listed on the license

If they can't pass this 30-second check, move on.

### Post 6: Problem/Solution (Week 3, Thu)
**Title:** "The $45K Question: Fixed-Price vs. Time-and-Materials"

Client question we get often: "Why fixed-price instead of T&M?"

**Time-and-Materials:**
- Client risk: Open-ended budget
- Contractor incentive: Slow down (more hours = more margin)
- Result: Projects slip, budgets balloon

**Fixed-Price (Our Model):**
- Client risk: Protected (budget locked)
- Contractor incentive: Finish on time (margin = fixed)
- Result: Project completes predictably, customer satisfied, repeat business

**The catch:** Fixed-price only works if you have a pre-vetted network and predictable timelines. If you're juggling subcontractors or guessing timelines, fixed-price is suicide.
