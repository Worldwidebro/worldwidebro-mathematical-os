# Teaching Guide for 7-Year-Olds

## Child Development at Age 7

**Cognitive:** Can focus 15-20 minutes, enjoys problem-solving, beginning logical thinking  
**Emotional:** Wants to please, competitive but learning sportsmanship, developing confidence  
**Physical:** Improving coordination, building strength, eager for active play  
**Social:** Making friendships, learning teamwork, developing independence  

---

## Core Teaching Principles

### 1. Make It Fun First
- Gamify learning (points, challenges, rewards)
- Mix play with instruction
- Celebrate effort, not just success
- Use humor and creativity

### 2. Show, Don't Tell
- Demonstrate before explaining
- Let him try immediately
- Narrate what you're doing: "See how I hold the controller? Now you try."
- Use your body and hands

### 3. Chunk Learning Into Small Steps
- One skill at a time
- Build progressively
- Review previous lessons briefly
- Don't overwhelm with information

### 4. Repetition With Variety
- Practice the same skill in different ways
- Increase difficulty gradually
- Mix fun practice with directed learning
- Celebrate improvement

### 5. Praise Effort & Growth
- "You worked really hard on that!"
- "I noticed you tried a different way—that's smart thinking"
- "You're getting better each time"
- Avoid: "You're so smart" (fixed mindset)

### 6. Make It Relevant to Him
- Connect to his interests (monsters, trucks, games)
- Let him choose when possible
- Show how skills help him achieve his goals
- Link new skills to existing knowledge

## Reading & Writing (Ages 7-8)

### Reading Level
- Beginning chapter books
- Sight words + phonics blending
- Reading for meaning, not just words
- 15-20 minutes comfortable reading time

### Teaching Strategies

**Letter Formation (Writing)**
- Practice 2-3 letters per session
- Use multi-sensory: write in sand, trace in air, form with playdough
- Point out letters in real life (Monster Truck signs, game menus)
- Praise fine motor effort

**Phonics**
- Start: CVC words (cat, dog, run)
- Build to: blends (st-, ch-, -ng)
- Introduce: sight words in context
- Practice: 10 minutes daily

**Reading Aloud**
- Take turns: you read 2 pages, he reads 1
- Ask comprehension questions as you go
- Let him pick books sometimes
- Make different voices fun

**Writing Ideas**
- Caption his drawings
- Write labels for his game worlds
- Simple sentence: "I like ___"
- Silly sentences: "The truck ate pizza"

## Roblox & Gaming

### Learning Opportunities
- **Problem-solving:** Find solutions in games
- **Persistence:** Keep trying when failing
- **Creativity:** Building worlds, designing games
- **Reading:** Game instructions, chat, world names
- **Teamwork:** Playing with friends

### Teaching While Playing
- Co-play for first 10 minutes, then let him lead
- Ask questions: "What are you trying to do? How will you do that?"
- Avoid controlling—let him figure things out
- Celebrate discoveries and creative ideas
- Use game concepts to teach: "That's like a real blueprint!"

### Building Skills in Roblox
- Start with simple games (obstacle courses, racing)
- Progress to building with platform tools
- Teach: Editing (modify objects), Grouping (combine parts), Color/Material
- Create: Personal space (room, headquarters, theme park section)

## Sports & Physical Skills

### Key Age-7 Skills
- Ball control (kicking, throwing, catching)
- Balance & coordination
- Running with purpose
- Following sports rules
- Good sportsmanship

### Teaching Methods
- **Demonstrate:** Show the skill first, slow it down
- **Practice:** Repetition in game-like context
- **Play:** Make it a game immediately (races, competitions, challenges)
- **Encourage:** Focus on effort and improvement
- **Rest:** Keep sessions 20-30 minutes with breaks

### Skills by Sport

**Basketball**
- Dribbling: Practice stationary first, then walking
- Shooting: Start close to basket, focus on form
- Defense: Teach blocking vs. stealing

**Soccer**
- Kicking: Inside foot for control, outside for power
- Trapping: Stop ball with foot/chest
- Passing: Accuracy before power

**Biking**
- Balance: Practice in open space without traffic
- Braking: Smooth, controlled stopping
- Steering: Wide turns, then tighter

## Life Skills & Character Building

### Problem-Solving
- "What's the problem?" (identify it)
- "What are some ways to fix it?" (brainstorm options)
- "Let's try this one" (pick & execute)
- "Did it work? What could we do differently?" (reflect)

### Resilience
- "It's okay to mess up. Everybody does."
- "Let's try again."
- "What did you learn?"
- Model failure + bounce-back for him

### Confidence Building
- Let him try new things
- Celebrate attempts, not just wins
- Give him responsibilities he can handle
- Ask his opinion: "What do you think we should do?"

### Following Directions
- Make eye contact
- Give one or two instructions (not five)
- Have him repeat back what you said
- Practice at home games first

---

## Attention & Focus

- **Ideal session length:** 20 minutes active learning
- **Breaks:** 5-10 minutes between focused activities
- **Flow:** Mix high-energy with calm activities
- **Transitions:** Give warning: "5 more minutes, then we stop"

---

## Your Role as Teacher-Dad

1. **Be present:** Put phone away, make eye contact, listen
2. **Be patient:** Progress takes time; celebrate small wins
3. **Be encouraging:** Find something to praise every session
4. **Be playful:** Laugh together; make it enjoyable
5. **Be consistent:** Practice same skills multiple times
6. **Be curious:** Ask questions about his thinking
7. **Be a model:** Show him you try new things and learn

---

## Quick Reference: 5-Minute Teaching Sessions

**Format:** Warm-up (2 min) → Skill (2 min) → Play (1 min)

- Warm-up: Chat, stretch, joke
- Skill: Show + demo, he tries 3-5 reps
- Play: Make it fun game right away

Example: "We're going to practice our basketball dribble. Watch me. Now you try. Good! Now let's race dribbling to the tree!"
