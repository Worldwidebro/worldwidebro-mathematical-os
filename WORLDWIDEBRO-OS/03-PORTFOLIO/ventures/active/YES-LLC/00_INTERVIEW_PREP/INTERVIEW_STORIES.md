# Interview Stories — STAR Method for Wave

**Interview Date**: June 5, 2026  
**Purpose**: Prepare 5 concrete stories to answer interview questions

---

## Why Stories Matter

**Don't say**: "I know automation, APIs, and databases"

**Do say**: "I automated a process that was taking 3 hours weekly, reducing it to 30 minutes, saving the team 10 hours monthly"

**People remember stories. Lists are forgotten.**

---

## The STAR Method

Each story has 4 parts:

- **Situation**: What was the context?
- **Task**: What were you responsible for?
- **Action**: What did you do? (Use "I")
- **Result**: What happened? (Quantify if possible)

---

## Your Core Contractor Story

**This is your strongest positioning:**

> "I take unclear business requirements, organize them into a clear plan, communicate expectations upfront, build the solution, document the work thoroughly, and deliver something the company can maintain and scale after I'm done."

Use this when asked: "Tell me about yourself"

---

## Story 1 — Solving a Technical Problem

**Question Wave Might Ask**: "Tell me about a technical challenge."

**Structure**:
- **Situation**: What was broken/slow/inefficient?
- **Task**: What were you asked to fix?
- **Action**: What steps did you take? (Analyze → Research → Test → Implement)
- **Result**: How much faster/more reliable/cheaper? (Use numbers)

**Template**: "[System] was [problem]. I [analyzed/researched/tested], [identified root cause], [implemented fix], and [measured result]. Performance improved by [X%]."

---

## Story 2 — Learning Something New

**Question**: "Tell me about learning a new technology."

**Structure**:
- **Situation**: You didn't know something you needed
- **Task**: Why did you need to learn it?
- **Action**: Documented → Researched → Built prototype → Tested → Implemented
- **Result**: Successfully delivered it

**Template**: "I hadn't worked with [technology] before, but [project required it]. I [learned it through X], [built a prototype], [tested it], and [delivered successfully]. [Quantified result]."

---

## Story 3 — Ambiguous Requirements

**Question**: "What do you do when requirements are unclear?"

**Structure**:
- **Situation**: Requirements were vague or conflicting
- **Task**: You were asked to deliver something unclear
- **Action**: Asked questions → Documented assumptions → Got alignment → Built according to spec
- **Result**: Delivered exactly what they needed (no rework)

**Template**: "A client wanted [vague requirement]. I asked [clarifying questions], documented [assumptions], got [stakeholder alignment], built [what was specified], and delivered [exactly what they needed]."

---

## Story 4 — Communication with Stakeholders

**Question**: "Tell me about working with different teams."

**Structure**:
- **Situation**: Different groups wanted different things
- **Task**: You needed to align them
- **Action**: Held meetings → Documented tradeoffs → Facilitated decision
- **Result**: Everyone understood and was satisfied

**Template**: "[Team A] wanted [X], [Team B] wanted [Y]. I [documented tradeoffs], [held sync], [got alignment], and we [decided together]. [Result: no conflict, on schedule, satisfied teams]."

---

## Story 5 — Automation and Efficiency

**Question**: "How do you improve processes?"

**Structure**:
- **Situation**: Manual process was inefficient
- **Task**: You were asked to improve it
- **Action**: Documented workflow → Identified repetitive steps → Designed automation → Implemented → Tested
- **Result**: X% time savings or X hours recovered

**Template**: "[Team] was [manually doing task] taking [time]. I [documented the workflow], [identified what to automate], [built integrations], [tested], and [automated it]. They now spend [reduced time]. That's [X hours saved]."

---

## How to Prepare Tonight

**Step 1** (15 min): Write out your 5 stories using real examples
**Step 2** (5 min): Read each aloud (should take 2-3 minutes each)
**Step 3** (5 min): Practice telling them naturally (not reading)

---

## Key Rules for Stories

✅ Use "I" statements (own your work)  
✅ Quantify results when possible  
✅ Keep each to 2-3 minutes  
✅ Be honest (don't exaggerate)  
✅ Connect back to Wave's needs  

❌ Don't ramble  
❌ Don't make excuses  
❌ Don't oversell  

---

## Connecting Stories to Wave

After telling a story, connect it to them:

> "With Wave's dispatch system, I'd approach it the same way — understand your current pain points, align everyone on what success looks like, build something maintainable, and deliver reliably."

---

## Practice Tonight

Write and practice. That's where confidence comes from.

When Wave asks about your approach, your story answers the question with proof, not claims.
