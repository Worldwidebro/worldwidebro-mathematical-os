## Call Strategy

### What You're Testing
1. **Pain point** — Do they struggle with payroll management?
2. **Frequency** — How often do they pay employees?
3. **Company size** — How many employees (20-200 range)?
4. **Current solution** — What are they using now? Satisfied?
5. **Willingness to pay** — Would $199/month solve the problem?
6. **Buy signals** — Timeline to decision, decision maker, budget

### Call Flow (20 minutes)
- **Intro (1 min)**: "Hi [Name], thanks for taking my call. We're building payroll software for small businesses. Quick questions?"
- **Discovery (12 min)**: Run through questions (below)
- **Pitch (5 min)**: "Here's what we built..." (show demo or describe)
- **Close (2 min)**: "Would this be interesting for you?" + next steps

## Discovery Call Script

### Opening (1 minute)
```
"Hi [Name], thanks so much for taking my call. My name is [Your Name]. 
We're building payroll software specifically for small construction and 
logistics companies, and I'm doing research to understand the biggest 
pain points.

Would you be willing to answer 15-20 quick questions about how you 
currently manage payroll? Shouldn't take more than 20 minutes."
```

---

### Discovery Questions (12 minutes)

#### Section 1: Current State (3 minutes)

**Q1**: "How do you currently process payroll?"
- Listen for: Spreadsheet, Quickbooks, ADP, Gusto, manual pen & paper, accountant handles it
- Follow-up if needed: "How long does it take each week?"

**Q2**: "How many employees do you have right now?"
- Listen for: Exact number, seasonal variation
- Note: Are they in target range (20-200)?

**Q3**: "How often do you pay employees? Weekly, biweekly, monthly?"
- Listen for: Weekly (higher pain), biweekly, monthly
- Note: Weekly = more pain = better customer fit

**Q4**: "Who manages payroll at your company?"
- Listen for: Owner, office manager, HR person, accountant
- Note: This is decision maker

---

#### Section 2: Pain Points (4 minutes)

**Q5**: "What's the most annoying part of payroll right now?"
- Listen for: Manual data entry, tax calculations, overtime tracking, direct deposit delays, compliance, tax filing
- Note: Top 2-3 pain points

**Q6**: "How much time does someone spend on payroll each week?"
- Listen for: Hours/week
- Note: Calculate cost = hours × hourly rate (for ROI)

**Q7**: "Have you made mistakes in payroll? What was the impact?"
- Listen for: Missed deadline, employee overpaid/underpaid, tax calculation error
- Note: Frequency + cost of errors

**Q8**: "What would the ideal payroll solution look like for you?"
- Listen for: Automation, fewer manual steps, better accuracy, faster turnaround, integration with their system
- Note: Feature priorities

---

#### Section 3: Current Solution Satisfaction (2 minutes)

**Q9**: "Are you happy with your current payroll solution?"
- Listen for: Yes, but... / No, because...
- Note: Dissatisfaction = opportunity

**Q10**: "What would you change about it?"
- Listen for: Cost, complexity, features missing, customer support
- Note: Competitive intel

---

#### Section 4: Buying Signal (3 minutes)

**Q11**: "If you could save 2-3 hours of payroll work per week, what would that be worth?"
- Listen for: $100-300/week value = $400-1200/month
- Note: Our $199/mo is 17-50% of value captured

**Q12**: "We're thinking about charging $199/month for a solution that handles all this. Would that be interesting for you?"
- Listen for: 
  - "Yes, definitely" = STRONG BUY SIGNAL
  - "Maybe, depends on X" = MEDIUM SIGNAL (note the X)
  - "Too expensive" = PRICE RESISTANCE (note number they'd pay)
  - "No" = LOW SIGNAL (but keep talking)

**Q13**: "If you were interested, what would be the timeline to make a decision? Weeks, months?"
- Listen for: ASAP (strong), Q2/Q3 (medium), "not sure" (weak)
- Note: Their buying timeline

**Q14**: "Would you be the person making the decision, or would you need to talk to [owner/CFO]?"
- Listen for: "Me" = easy, "I'd check with X" = harder
- Note: Decision maker name

---

### Pitch (5 minutes)

**Setup**:
"Based on what you've told me, here's what we're building..."

**Demo** (2 approaches):
1. **Live demo** (if they seem interested): Show HRMS on your laptop
   - Employee list + timesheet entry
   - Automatic paycheck calculation
   - Direct deposit setup
   - Tax forms (W-4, paystubs)

2. **Verbal description** (if on phone):
   "Basically: employees clock in hours online, system automatically calculates taxes, direct deposit happens, you're done. No spreadsheets, no manual math, no guessing. Takes maybe 10 minutes per pay period."

**Key points**:
- "Designed for companies like you (20-200 people)"
- "Works with construction + field services (we'll start there)"
- "Starts at $199/month"
- "First customer launch in June"

---

### Close (2 minutes)

**If interested** ("Yes" or "Maybe"):
"Great! Here's what I'd suggest: We're launching in June, and we're looking for 5-10 beta customers to test with. Would you be interested in trying the beta for free, or at least seeing a demo?"

**Action**: Get email, add to beta list

**If not interested** ("No"):
"I totally understand. Can I ask—what would make it more interesting for you? [Listen]. OK, thanks for your time. If we address [X], can I circle back in a few months?"

**Either way**:
- "Would you know anyone else in [industry] I should talk to?"
- "Thanks so much for your time. Really appreciate it."

## Expected Outcomes & Patterns

### Strong Signal (Buy Signal 4-5)
- Pain point clear (3+ hours/week) and costly
- Current solution inadequate
- $199/mo is fair or cheap
- Timeline < 1 month
- Willing to beta test
- **Action**: Add to beta/early access list

### Medium Signal (Buy Signal 2-3)
- Some pain points, but not urgent
- Might be open if price is right / feature is specific
- Timeline 3-6 months
- **Action**: Keep on list, follow up in 6 weeks

### Weak Signal (Buy Signal 1)
- Not currently a pain
- Happy with existing solution
- Price too high
- Timeline unclear/far away
- **Action**: Thank them, take referral if available, move on

## What NOT to Do

- ❌ Don't pitch for 15+ minutes (listen, not tell)
- ❌ Don't ask loaded questions ("Wouldn't you love if payroll took 5 minutes?")
- ❌ Don't promise features they ask for (say "we'll consider it")
- ❌ Don't give them false hope (be honest about timeline)
- ❌ Don't skip companies that seem "not interested" (they might refer someone)
